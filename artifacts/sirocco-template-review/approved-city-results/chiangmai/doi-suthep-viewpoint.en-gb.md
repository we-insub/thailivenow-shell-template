# Wat Phra That Doi Suthep Viewpoint

## Introduction

Fifteen kilometres west of Chiang Mai, on a mountaintop 1,073 metres above sea level, stands a temple founded in 1383. Its golden chedi rises 22 metres and enshrines a Buddha relic; climb the 306-step staircase guarded by naga serpents on both sides and you reach the temple terrace. From the terrace, the whole of Chiang Mai spreads out below — worship and panorama in a single visit.

In the early morning (6–8 am), monks' chanting fills the air and the crowds are thin, so it feels serene. Toward sunset, the golden chedi turns red and the city lights begin to come on. Climb the 306 steps or ride the tram up, take in the view from the terrace, then circle the chedi clockwise in worship — a half-day itinerary of 2–3 hours works well.

## Public transport / Grab·Bolt

- Shared songthaew (red truck): departs from Chang Phueak Gate / near Chiang Mai Zoo (Huai Kaeo Rd), approx. 40–60 baht one way (third-party information)
- Grab·Bolt: can be hailed, but availability is patchy on mountain roads
- Rental car / motorbike: via Route 1004 (Huai Kaeo–Sriwichai Rd); winding mountain road, driving experience needed

## Taxi phrases

## Why visit

### Worship and city panorama in one stop

A mountaintop temple founded in 1383, a 22-metre golden chedi enshrining a Buddha relic, and the Chiang Mai city panorama from the terrace — all in one place. Well suited to travellers interested in Buddhist culture and history.

### Climbing the 306 naga steps

The climb itself, up 306 steps flanked by naga serpent statues, is part of the experience. As the story goes, a white elephant carrying a Buddha relic climbed this mountain, trumpeted three times and collapsed — and the temple was built on that spot. If the steps feel too much, take the tram (cable car) instead.

### Early-morning chanting or sunset

Early morning (6–8 am) is quiet, with chanting and few visitors; at sunset the chedi turns red as the city lights come on. If you enjoy photography, aim for the terrace at dusk. With family, plan on the tram given children's stamina.

### Worth reconsidering if

Rain blurs the terrace views, midday heat makes the steps gruelling, and peak-season midday crowds are best avoided.

## FAQ

**Q. Is there an admission fee?**
A. Per third-party guidance: 30 baht for foreigners, about 20 baht for the tram. The temple's official channels haven't confirmed this — check the on-site rates on the day.

**Q. What are the opening hours?**
A. The common third-party figure is daily 06:00–20:00. Without official confirmation, keep your schedule flexible.

**Q. Do I have to climb the 306 steps?**
A. No. You can ride the tram (cable car) up — about 20 baht per third-party figures.

**Q. What should I wear?**
A. As a temple, modest dress covering shoulders and knees is the baseline (common third-party guidance). Remove your shoes in worship areas.

**Q. Can I go on a rainy day?**
A. Worship is fine, but the terrace views turn hazy. A clear day is better.

**Q. How do I get back?**
A. Take a shared songthaew waiting at the car park or hail a Grab. Availability is patchy in the mountains, so allow plenty of time for the descent.

# Wat Phra That Doi Suthep Viewpoint

## Introduction

Fifteen kilometres west of Chiang Mai, on a mountaintop 1,073 metres above sea level, stands a temple founded in 1383. Its golden chedi rises 22 metres and enshrines a Buddha relic; climb the 306-step staircase guarded by naga serpents on both sides and you reach the temple terrace. From the terrace, the whole of Chiang Mai spreads out below — worship and panorama in a single visit.

Early in the morning (6–8 am), the monks' chanting fills the air and the crowds are thin, so it feels peaceful. Towards sunset, the golden chedi turns red and the city lights start coming on. Climb the 306 steps or take the tram up, enjoy the view from the terrace, then go around the chedi clockwise in worship — a half-day plan of 2–3 hours works well.

## Public transport / Grab·Bolt

- Shared songthaew (red truck): leaves from Chang Phueak Gate / near Chiang Mai Zoo (Huai Kaeo Rd), roughly 40–60 baht one way (third-party information)
- Grab·Bolt: can be booked, but availability keeps fluctuating on the mountain roads
- Rental car / motorbike: via Route 1004 (Huai Kaeo–Sriwichai Rd); winding ghat road, you need driving experience

## Taxi phrases

## Why visit

### Darshan and city panorama in one go

A mountaintop temple from 1383, a 22-metre golden chedi with a Buddha relic inside, and the Chiang Mai city panorama from the terrace — all in one place. Good for travellers interested in Buddhist culture and history.

### Climbing the 306 naga steps

The climb up 306 steps lined with naga serpent statues on both sides is itself worth it. The story goes that a white elephant carrying a Buddha relic climbed this hill, trumpeted three times and collapsed — the temple was built on that very spot. If the steps feel too much, just take the tram (cable car) up.

### Early-morning chanting or sunset time

Early morning (6–8 am) is quiet with chanting and few people; at sunset the chedi turns red as the city lights come on. If you like photography, target the terrace at dusk. With family, plan for the tram keeping children's stamina in mind.

### Think twice if

Rain makes the terrace views hazy, midday heat makes the steps exhausting, and peak-season midday crowds are better avoided.

## FAQ

**Q. Is there any entry fee?**
A. As per third-party guidance: 30 baht for foreigners, about 20 baht for the tram. The temple's official channels have not confirmed this — please check the rates at the counter on the day.

**Q. What are the timings?**
A. The common third-party figure is daily 06:00–20:00. With no official confirmation, keep your plan flexible.

**Q. Is climbing the 306 steps compulsory?**
A. No. You can take the tram (cable car) up — about 20 baht as per third-party figures.

**Q. What should I wear?**
A. It is a temple, so modest dress covering shoulders and knees is the basic rule (common third-party guidance). Remove your footwear in worship areas.

**Q. Can I go on a rainy day?**
A. Darshan is fine, but the terrace views turn hazy. A clear day is better.

**Q. How do I return?**
A. Take a shared songthaew waiting at the parking lot or book a Grab. Availability is patchy in the hills, so keep enough time for coming down.

# Phi Phi Viewpoint 2

## Introduction

The most famous view on Phi Phi isn't from the sea — it's from the hill above it. Stand on the terrace of Viewpoint 2 and Tonsai Bay and Loh Dalum Bay face each other across a narrow sandy isthmus. The twin-bay panorama from the postcards, right in front of you. In person it's more three-dimensional than any photo — the emerald bays spreading on both sides and the village rooftops strung along the sandbar, almost too wide for a single frame.

If you're here to take photos, come between 7 and 11 in the morning. The sun sits behind you, so the twin bays come out crisp with no harsh backlight. With fewer people around, you can set up a tripod without feeling in the way. If you're here for the sunset, head up after 4 PM. The 30 minutes when the western sky turns red and both bays go gold are this viewpoint's highlight.

Two hours is plenty. Thirty to forty minutes up, a good hour on the terrace for photos and a drink at the café on top. If you stay for sunset, a torch for the way down is a must.

## Getting there

There are no trains or buses on Phi Phi. Take a ferry from Phuket (Rassada Pier), Krabi (Klong Jilad Pier) or Ao Nang to Tonsai Pier, then walk. No cars can drive on the island, so Grab and Bolt car-hailing don't work here. To move between beaches, hop on a longtail boat.

The fastest way up is the stair route: walk 10 minutes from Tonsai Pier toward Harmony House and follow the "Viewpoint" / "Tsunami Evacuation Route" signs. Past Phi Phi Arboreal Resort, steep stairs climb to Viewpoint 1 (the ticket office); from there it's another 15–20 minutes to Viewpoint 2. All in all, 30–40 minutes. If you'd rather take it slow, there's a gentler detour north of the village (about 60 minutes) or a route starting from Loh Dalum Bay (about 30 minutes).

Head back down the same way. After sunset the trail has no lighting at all, so start descending before dark — and bring a torch and mosquito repellent.

There are no taxis on Phi Phi. Show these to a longtail-boat operator or hotel staff if you need to.

## Why visit

**The twin-bay panorama in crisp morning light**
From the terrace, Tonsai Bay and Loh Dalum Bay spread out on both sides of the sandbar. Between 7 and 11 AM the sun is behind you, so photos come out at their clearest with no backlight to fight. Fewer people means you can take your time framing the shot.

**Thirty golden minutes at sunset**
Head up after 4 PM to watch the western sky turn red and both bays go gold. It's the busiest time of day, so claim a good spot a little early. You'll need a torch for the way down.

**343 steps and a jungle trail — a light hike**
The 343 steps up to Viewpoint 1 and the jungle path are part of the experience. The panorama at the top feels bigger for the sweat. Prefer a slower pace? The gentler northern detour (about 60 minutes) is there too.

## A great fit for these trips — and days to think twice about

It's a great fit if you're spending a day or two on Phi Phi and want the island's signature view properly seen, if you've brought a camera, or if you want the reward to match the sweat.

On the other hand, think twice on rainy days or right after rain — the steps get slippery and the whole climb stays tense. If your knees or stamina aren't up to it, 343 steep steps can be a lot. Strollers and wheelchairs can't do the stair sections at all.

## FAQ

**How much is the ticket?**
50 baht. Buy it at the Viewpoint 1 ticket office and it covers Viewpoints 1 and 2 together — no reservation needed. Viewpoint 3 is run separately and costs 20 baht on its own.

**When's the best time to go?**
For photos, 7–11 AM — the sun is behind you so there's no harsh backlight. For sunset, head up after 4 PM. After dark there's no lighting, so bring a torch.

**Can I bring children?**
They can make the climb, but hold their hand on the 343 steep steps. Strollers can't do the stairs.

**How is it different from Viewpoints 1 and 3?**
Viewpoint 1 is the gateway at the top of the 343 steps, with the ticket office and the "I LOVE PHI PHI" sign. Viewpoint 2 is the main twin-bay panorama point with a small café. Viewpoint 3 is a quieter, separately run viewpoint that costs 20 baht on its own.

**Where do I start the climb?**
Fastest is the stair route from Tonsai Pier toward Harmony House — 30–40 minutes. Alternatives go via Bob's Bar from Loh Dalum Bay (about 30 minutes) or the northern detour (about 60 minutes).

**What if it rains?**
The steps get slippery and dangerous. Skip rainy days, and wear non-slip shoes even after rain.

# U.S. Travelers to Thailand: Airport Counters, Flight Certificates, Baggage Claims, and Travel Insurance Guide

## Getting to Thailand from the U.S.: one honest fact first

There is no nonstop flight between the United States and Thailand. Not from Los Angeles, New York, San Francisco, or anywhere else — as of October 13, 2026, every U.S.–Thailand airport pair we verified (352 pairs) is served only with a connection. Do not buy an itinerary that claims otherwise.

United Airlines is the only U.S. carrier flying to Bangkok, and even it does not fly nonstop. United's UA820/821 runs daily between Los Angeles (LAX) and Bangkok (BKK) with a Hong Kong (HKG) stop — a one-stop trip under a single flight number. UA820 leaves Los Angeles at 23:15 and lands in Bangkok at 11:25; the return UA821 leaves Bangkok at 16:55 and lands in Los Angeles at 20:25 (Tourism Authority of Thailand, October 2025). Thai Airways has no U.S. service at all: its Bangkok–Los Angeles route ended on October 25, 2015, and Bangkok–New York (JFK) in 2008. American Airlines has no Thailand service either.

Why this matters for the rest of this guide: most U.S. travelers to Thailand fly Thai Airways only as a connection (through Tokyo, Taipei, Seoul, or Hong Kong) or onward within Thailand — so this page covers Thai Airways' Bangkok counters and paperwork, and United's U.S.-side processes, rather than pretending either flies you nonstop.

## At Bangkok Suvarnabhumi (BKK): ticketing vs. check-in counters

**Check-in counters (owner-confirmed from Thai Airways' official airport information page, October 13, 2026):**

- International economy class: rows **H6–H17**
- Royal Silk (business class): rows **A3–A10**
- Self baggage drop: **D5–D9** and **J5–J19**
- Check-in opens **8 hours** before departure and closes **50 minutes** before departure. Checked bags must be dropped at least 60 minutes before departure.
- Confirm your row on the departure-hall flight boards — Thai Airways does not publish per-flight row assignments.
- Thai Airways BKK office: 4th floor, **+66-2132-1888**. International Lost & Found: **+66-2134-5453~56**, 24 hours.
- General reservations and inquiries: THAI Customer Contact Center **+66 2 356-1111**.

These row numbers (H6–17, A3–10, D5–9, J5–19) supersede older row data — do not use the older J/H/C row references you may find in older FAQs.

**Ticketing vs. check-in — they are not the same desk.** Check-in counters handle same-day departure, bag drop, and boarding passes. Ticketing counters (or offices) handle new bookings, exchanges, and reissues. For Thai Airways, ticket changes are directed to the THAI office in your departure city. Note that the THAI Shop at Suvarnabhumi's OPC Center (tel 02 137 0175) is a souvenir shop, not a ticketing counter.

**United Airlines at BKK:** United does not publish fixed counter row numbers at Suvarnabhumi. Use the flight information boards in the departures hall, or ask United staff. United's international check-in closes 60 minutes before departure.

## Finding your counter: a practical walkthrough

Check-in counters live on the departures level — at Suvarnabhumi, that is Level 4. When you enter the hall, look up: each row is signed by letter and number (H6–H17 for Thai Airways economy, for example). If you cannot find your airline's row, use the big flight information boards, which list counter rows per flight.

For the airport itself rather than an airline: Airports of Thailand runs a 24-hour Contact Center at **1722**, and Suvarnabhumi's main line is **+66 2 132 1888**. AOT does not publish an airline-by-airline counter directory, so for counter-specific questions ask the airline's own staff.

At U.S. airports, United's counters are reported at Los Angeles International's Terminal 7/8 — this comes from third-party terminal guides and has not been verified on the airport's official channel, so reconfirm on the airport's flight boards or on united.com before you go.

## Delay and cancellation certificates: the document your insurer wants

U.S. travel insurance usually pays travel-delay benefits only with written proof that your flight was delayed or cancelled. Secure that proof at the time it happens, not weeks later:

- **Thai Airways:** Use the official **"Request Flight Disruption"** form on thaiairways.com. It exists specifically for passengers who need a document for insurance purposes after a delay, cancellation, or diversion — fill in your flight details and request the certificate (verified by direct page open, October 13, 2026).
- **United:** United is reported to provide verification letters on request — email **delayletter@united.com** with the names of everyone in your party, your confirmation number, flight numbers and travel dates, a phone contact, and the email or mailing address where the letter should go. Allow 5–10 business days. (Quoted from United's own "Notification of delays and cancellations" page as cited by a frequent-flyer forum; not re-verified by a direct text fetch of united.com, so confirm availability.)
- Back it up yourself: keep boarding passes, rebooking emails, and screenshots of flight-status pages.

**Know your U.S. refund rights separately.** Under the U.S. Department of Transportation's automatic refund rule, a cancelled or significantly changed flight to or from the U.S. — international flights delayed or moved 6+ hours, airport changes, added connections, or a class downgrade — entitles you to an **automatic cash refund** if you do not rebook. No request needed; airlines must pay within 7 business days for credit card purchases or 20 days otherwise, and may not substitute vouchers unless you affirmatively accept them. The U.S. does **not** have a general cash-compensation rule beyond refunds, so your travel insurance policy is the main source of delay reimbursement — which is exactly why the airline's delay letter matters.

## Baggage: PIR, damage, and lost bags

File a Property Irregularity Report (PIR) at the airline's baggage desk in the arrivals hall **before you leave the airport**. This is the foundation of every later claim — with the airline and with your insurer.

- **Thai Airways:** You get a report copy plus a File Reference Number. The written claim (PIR plus receipts) must reach Thai Airways within **21 days** of delivery. For Bangkok: **baggage.service@thaiairways.com** or **baggageclaim@thaiairways.com**. International Lost & Found at BKK: **+66-2134-5453~56**, 24 hours.
- **United:** Report delayed or damaged bags at the destination airport. United delivers bags through Bags VIP at 250+ U.S. airports and grants an **automatic bag fee refund in some cases when a bag is delayed over 12 hours**. If a Bags VIP delivery is disrupted by a cancellation or rerouting, call **1-877-847-0045**. U.S. DOT rules also entitle you to a checked-bag-fee refund if a bag is significantly delayed (15 or 30 hours for international flights, depending on flight length) once you have filed a mishandled baggage report.
- Photograph your bags, baggage tags, and any damage, and keep every receipt for necessary replacement items.

## Travel insurance claim documents: what U.S. travelers need

Pack a small claim kit when you travel: the airline's delay/cancellation letter or certificate, your booking confirmation (PNR) and itinerary, boarding passes, the PIR if bags are involved, itemized receipts for necessary purchases, and time-stamped photos of damage.

As a real U.S. example of how policies work: United Vacations' Travel Protection Plus — underwritten by **Generali U.S. Branch** — reimburses up to **$1,000 per person** for reasonable travel-delay expenses after a **6+ hour** delay from covered events such as a common carrier delay, and up to **$300 per person** for necessary items when luggage is delayed 6+ hours en route. But every plan differs by state, policy terms, exclusions, and proof requirements, and the full plan document always governs. Read your own policy before you fly; do not assume another market's policy wording applies to U.S. travel.

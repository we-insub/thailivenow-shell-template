# India–Thailand Flights: Airports, Counters, and Disruption Help for Indian Travellers

Planning a trip from India to Thailand? This page brings together what Indian travellers most need at the airport end: which airlines fly direct from your city, where to check in at Bangkok, Phuket and Krabi airports, which phone numbers actually work for each purpose, how to report damaged baggage, how to get a delay certificate for your travel insurance, and what documents Indian insurers typically ask for. Every claim below is tied to an official source — the airline's own website, the airport operator, or an Indian government authority.

## Direct routes from India to Thailand

Four Thai airports receive direct (non-stop) flights from India as of 13 October 2026: **Bangkok Suvarnabhumi (BKK)**, **Bangkok Don Mueang (DMK)**, **Phuket (HKT)** and **Krabi (KBV)**. Bangkok has two international airports — BKK and DMK are different airports in different parts of the city, so always confirm which one your flight uses before heading out.

From Delhi (DEL), Mumbai (BOM), Kolkata (CCU), Chennai (MAA), Bengaluru (BLR) and Hyderabad (HYD), you can fly non-stop to Bangkok's Suvarnabhumi on a mix of IndiGo, Air India and Thai Airways; Thai Airways also serves Ahmedabad directly. Don Mueang (DMK) is the low-cost base: Thai AirAsia X flies Delhi–DMK, Thai Lion Air flies Delhi and Amritsar to DMK, and Thai AirAsia resumes six India routes — Gaya, Ahmedabad, Guwahati, Hyderabad, Lucknow and Jaipur — from late October and November 2026 (none of these had restarted as of 13 October 2026). Phuket gets direct flights from Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata and Bengaluru on IndiGo, and Krabi gets Delhi on IndiGo. Nok Air's international flights were suspended in August 2025 with no restart. IndiGo's Gaya–Bangkok and Guwahati–Bangkok services launch in late October 2026, after this page's verification date.

Route entries in this guide are included only where the non-stop service is confirmed by official airline, airport, or government sources. Flight numbers appear only where the airline itself published them (IndiGo); where official channels confirm the service but publish no flight number, this is stated explicitly. Always re-confirm your flight's schedule in the airline's own booking engine or app close to departure.

## Ticketing vs check-in counters: who has what at the airport

A common frustration at Thai airports: you arrive, look for a ticketing counter, and find none. In practice, India–Thailand carriers sell tickets online or through their apps, and airport counter space is for check-in, bag drop and day-of-travel service.

Confirmed counter positions from official sources:

- **Thai Airways at Suvarnabhumi (BKK):** economy class check-in at counters **H6–H17**, Royal Silk (business) at **A3–A10**, online baggage drop at **D5–D9 / J5–J19**, on the Level 4 departures hall (Thai Airways official airport-information page). Check-in opens **8 hours** before departure and closes **50 minutes** before; checked baggage must be handed over at least **60 minutes** before departure. Note that the THAI Shop at Suvarnabhumi (OPC Center) is a souvenir shop, not a ticketing counter.
- **Thai AirAsia X at Don Mueang (DMK):** Terminal 1 (international terminal), **Level 3, counters 6–7**, open from 3 hours before departure (AirAsia official newsroom and support page).
- **Phuket (HKT) baggage desks:** the AOT official arrival procedure places baggage service desks in the international arrivals hall on Level 1 — Thai Airways near baggage belt No. 5, and AOTGA near baggage belt No. 1.

For **IndiGo, Air India, Air India Express, SpiceJet, Thai Vietjet, Thai AirAsia (FD), Thai Lion Air**, no fixed ticketing or check-in counter rows at Thai airports were published on the airlines' official channels as of 13 October 2026. Their counters are assigned on the day of travel and shown on the airport's flight information displays and on your boarding pass. If you need to buy or change a ticket, use the airline's website or app; at the airport, head to the airline's service desk in the departures hall.

## Phone numbers and what each one is for

Using the right number saves a long wait. Confirmed numbers from official sources:

- **Air India, India Customer Care (24×7):** +91 116 932 9333 or +91 116 932 9999 — reservations, flight changes, baggage queries, refunds. Standard call charges apply. These are call-centre lines, not direct lines to airport counters.
- **IndiGo customer care:** +91 124 4973838 (alternate: +91 124 6173838) — published in IndiGo's own Conditions of Carriage. Again, a call centre, not an airport counter.
- **Thai Airways Contact Center:** +66 2 356-1111 — reservations and general enquiries. At BKK, the airline's office is on the 4th floor (+66-2132-1888), and International Lost & Found is at +66-2134-5453~56 (24 hours). These are airline lines, not direct counter lines.
- **Airport operator (AOT):** 1722, 24 hours — general airport help, not airline matters.

For grievances with Air India, the official escalation path is: raise a Case ID on the airline's customer support portal, then the Nodal Officer (nodalofficer@airindia.com), then the Appellate Authority (appellateauthority@airindia.com); passengers may also lodge grievances on the AirSewa portal.

## Finding your counter on the day

No carrier publishes a fixed counter row for its India stations except the cases above, so the counter number comes from the **flight information displays** and your boarding pass. At BKK, international departures check in on Level 4 (rows A–W across 460 counters); Thai Airways uses H6–H17 (economy), A3–A10 (Royal Silk), D5–D9/J5–J19 (online baggage drop). At DMK, Thai AirAsia X is at Terminal 1, Level 3, counters 6–7. Arrive with margin: counters typically open 3 hours before departure and close around an hour before (Thai Airways: opens 8 hours before, closes 50 minutes before).

## Damaged, delayed or lost baggage: filing the PIR

If your checked bag is delayed, lost or arrives damaged: **report it to the airline's ground staff at the arrival airport before you leave the arrivals hall** and file a Property Irregularity Report (PIR). Keep the PIR reference number, your baggage tag, boarding pass and receipts for anything essential you buy. Without a complaint filed before leaving, the airline may treat the bag as delivered in good condition.

Deadlines from official carriage terms: **IndiGo** requires delayed-baggage claims to be notified within 21 days from the date the bag is placed at your disposal; **Thai Airways** requires a written claim within 21 days of delivery, after immediate reporting at the arrival airport. IndiGo lets you track lost-baggage status on its website. At Phuket, the official AOT arrival procedure puts the Thai Airways baggage desk near belt No. 5 and the AOTGA desk near belt No. 1, on the Level 1 international arrivals hall.

## Delay and cancellation certificates

Indian travellers most often need a written delay or cancellation certificate for travel insurance claims (baggage delay, trip delay) or expense claims. Airline-by-airline:

- **IndiGo** runs an official online **Travel Certificate portal** — request with your PNR/booking reference and email; certificates are charged per passenger per sector, and older records go through the 24/7 call centre.
- **Thai Airways'** official Help page links a Flight Disruption Certificate section, but the issue steps were not confirmed on an official page — ask ground staff at the departure airport for written confirmation of the disruption.
- **Air India, Thai Vietjet, Thai AirAsia (X), Thai Lion Air, Air India Express, SpiceJet:** no official online certificate procedure was found on official channels. Ask the airline's ground staff at the departure airport for written confirmation at the time, and keep your boarding pass, booking reference and delay notifications.

## Your rights under India's DGCA rules

India's **Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA)** sets passenger rights in Civil Aviation Requirement (CAR) Section 3, Series M, Part IV — mirrored in the Ministry of Civil Aviation's Passenger Charter. These rules cover flights to, from and within India, so they apply to India–Thailand flights on Indian **and** Thai carriers. In short: on cancellation the airline must provide an alternate flight or compensation plus a full refund, and meals if you have already reported at the airport; on delay the airline must provide meals and refreshments, and depending on the delay an alternate flight or full refund, or hotel accommodation with transfers. No compensation is owed when the disruption is caused by extraordinary circumstances (force majeure) beyond the airline's control. Complaints go first to the airline, then to the **AirSewa** app/portal, and then to statutory bodies or courts if unresolved.

## Indian travel insurance: documents you'll typically need

Do not rely on generic checklists — each Indian insurer's policy wording decides. But IRDAI-filed travel insurance policy wordings consistently ask for: the completed and signed claim form; the original ticket/boarding pass or a passport copy showing your travel dates; all original bills, vouchers and receipts; and for baggage-delay claims, the **PIR from the airline** stating scheduled and actual delivery times. Medical claims need the doctor's notes, admission/discharge cards, itemised bills with prescriptions, diagnostic reports, and passport/visa copies with entry and exit stamps. Loss of passport needs an FIR/final police report. A delay certificate and a PIR do not by themselves guarantee a payout — the insurer decides under your policy's terms, exclusions and time limits.

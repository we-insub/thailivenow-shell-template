# Direct flights from the UK to Thailand: airlines, airport counters and disruption help

If you are flying from the UK to Thailand, you have a genuine choice of direct options — but the picture is livelier than most search results suggest, and it changes with the seasons. This guide covers the UK-Thailand routes confirmed from airline-official sources as of 13 October 2026, where each airline checks in, how to reach the right people, what to do about baggage, and which documents to collect for a UK travel-insurance claim. Facts are checked against airline and airport official pages on 13 October 2026; where something could not be confirmed, this guide says so instead of guessing.

## Who flies direct from the UK to Thailand?

Six airport pairings currently link Britain and Thailand, and every one of them is confirmed by the operating airline's own published material.

**London Heathrow to Bangkok** is Thai Airways territory: TG911 and TG917 fly Heathrow Terminal 2 to Bangkok Suvarnabhumi, with TG910 and TG916 on the return. It is the only carrier still flying the Heathrow–Bangkok pairing, after British Airways moved its Bangkok service to Gatwick. Thai Airways' own fares page showed bookable Bangkok–London round trips for travel through March 2027, refreshed within hours of our check.

**London Gatwick to Bangkok** is the busiest corridor. British Airways flies it year-round — three times a week in summer 2026, rising to six a week in winter 2026–27, per BA's own schedule announcement — and Norse Atlantic operates it seasonally with its Dreamliners: three times a week from October 2026, increasing to four a week from December through mid-April 2027, after a planned summer pause. BA checks in from a dedicated Zone A area in Gatwick's South Terminal.

**Manchester to Bangkok** is Norse Atlantic's route and the first-ever direct link between the two cities. It ran once a week in winter 2025–26 and the airline's official winter announcement confirms an expansion to four flights a week from December 2026; the expanded timetable had not been published at the time of checking.

**London Gatwick to Phuket** has two operators. TUI Airways runs winter-season charter flights, listed in its official Thailand flight guide, and Norse Atlantic announced a brand-new three-times-weekly Gatwick–Phuket service starting December 2026 through mid-April 2027 — announced but not yet flying when we checked, with no flight numbers published.

**Manchester to Phuket** and **Birmingham to Phuket** are TUI Airways winter charters, both listed in TUI's official Thailand flight guide. TUI publishes its season flight details inside its booking system rather than on a public timetable, so exact flight numbers and timings are confirmed at booking.

One absence to note: EVA Air's BR67/68 London–Bangkok operation appears on independent flight trackers, but we could not confirm it from any EVA official source on 13 October 2026, so it is treated as unverified rather than presented as a bookable service. And British Airways' old Heathrow–Bangkok flights BA9/BA10 are withdrawn — do not expect to find them.

If you are departing from Edinburgh, Glasgow, Newcastle, Stansted, Luton, Bristol or Cardiff, there is no direct service to Thailand confirmed from any airline's published material: every checked itinerary from those airports connects somewhere first.

## Ticketing desks versus check-in counters: what the difference means for you

A ticketing desk sells or changes tickets; a check-in counter handles your departure. Airlines increasingly separate the two, and Thailand is no exception.

**Thai Airways at Bangkok Suvarnabhumi** publishes a full counter map on its official airport-information page: international economy at H6–H17, Royal Silk at A3–A10, and online-baggage counters at D5–D9 and J5–J19. Check-in opens 8 hours before departure, closes 50 minutes before, and bags are accepted from 60 minutes before departure at the earliest. Its official FAQ separately lists internet check-in counters J1–J5/H1–H5 for international flights (up to 60 minutes before departure) and domestic bag-drop counter C1 (up to 45 minutes before). THAI's FAQ states ticket changes are handled at the departure city's THAI office — and its THAI Shop at Suvarnabhumi's OPC Centre is a souvenir shop, not a ticketing counter, so don't queue there for ticket changes. At Heathrow, THAI's official training material places check-in in Terminal 2 (The Queen's Terminal), Zone A, Level 5; that material dates from 2019, so reconfirm before you travel.

**EVA Air at Bangkok** does not keep a ticketing desk inside the terminal. Its published ticketing counter address is its city office — 725 S-METRO Building, 17th Floor, Room 1700, Sukhumvit Road, Bangkok, open Monday to Friday 08:30–17:30, phone +66-2-3027300. Its reservation and ticketing hotline is +66-2-4609188 (Chinese/English, 24 hours). For check-in, EVA publishes no fixed counter rows; check the departure-day airport screens, and note its self-service baggage drop runs from 6 hours to 1 hour before departure.

**British Airways at London Gatwick** operates from the South Terminal with a dedicated check-in area in Zone A, a short walk from the Gatwick Airport railway station — 29 check-in kiosks and 12 self-service bag drops, with a separate space for premium customers. Whether BA keeps a separate new-booking desk inside the terminal could not be confirmed from BA's official pages.

**TUI Airways** sells through its website, app and UK high-street shops, and publishes no fixed check-in counter numbers for Gatwick, Manchester or Birmingham; counter allocation appears on departure screens on the day.

**Norse Atlantic's** counter allocations and contact channels could not be confirmed from official sources in this session, and a dedicated verification task is running separately. Until then, rely on your booking confirmation and the airport's flight information.

## Baggage problems: the PIR and damage claims

File a Property Irregularity Report before you leave the arrivals hall — it is the document UK insurers expect to see.

**Thai Airways** (official delayed-baggage page): report to the airline's ground staff at the arrival airport immediately; the airline gives you a report copy with a File Reference Number. Then send a written claim within 21 days of the baggage being handed over, with the PIR and receipts attached. For Bangkok claims the page lists baggage.service@thaiairways.com and baggageclaim@thaiairways.com. At Suvarnabhumi, the international Lost and Found line is **+66-2134-5453~56** (24 hours).

**EVA Air** (official baggage page): report immediately to the local airport office or ground handler. For partial loss or damage, claim within 7 days of receiving the baggage; for delayed or totally lost baggage, within 21 days of the scheduled arrival date.

For **British Airways, TUI and Norse Atlantic**, no airline-specific official PIR counter location could be confirmed — report to the airline's handling desk at the arrival airport on the day.

## Delayed or cancelled? Certificates and your UK rights

For British Airways, TUI Airways, EVA Air and Thai Airways, no official published procedure for written delay or cancellation certificates could be found on the airlines' own channels as of 13 October 2026 — a check across 35 airlines' official channels found nothing. For Norse Atlantic the picture is unverified. In practice: ask the airline's ground staff at the airport for written confirmation of the reason and timings on the day.

Keep everything that proves what happened: booking reference, flight number and date, scheduled and actual arrival times, delay or cancellation notices, and receipts for reasonable expenses. Under UK passenger-rights rules, a cancelled covered flight normally entitles you to choose between a refund and re-routing, and fixed compensation depends on distance and arrival delay when the disruption was within the airline's control — extraordinary circumstances can remove compensation, but never the duty of care (meals, accommodation). The UK Civil Aviation Authority's official guidance on flight delays and cancellations is the reference point; if the airline rejects your claim, escalation routes include an approved alternative dispute resolution provider or the CAA's Passenger Advice and Complaints Team.

## Documents for a UK travel-insurance claim

This section uses only UK official and government-backed sources. The FCDO's foreign travel insurance guidance says: take your policy details with you, including the policy number and the insurer's emergency assistance telephone number, and give a copy to your travel companions and family back home in case they need to contact your insurer in an emergency. MoneyHelper, the UK's government-backed money guidance service, advises that some insurers insist on receipts as proof for baggage items, so keep purchase receipts from the start.

Before you leave the airport, gather: the airline's PIR for any baggage problem, boarding passes and baggage tags, the airline's written confirmation of any delay or cancellation, original receipts for out-of-pocket expenses, photos of damage, proof of purchase for lost or damaged items, a police report for theft, and medical certificates for illness or injury claims. Policy terms differ between UK insurers — excesses, delay thresholds and exclusions vary — so these documents support a claim but do not guarantee one, and filing a claim is not the same as a payout decision.

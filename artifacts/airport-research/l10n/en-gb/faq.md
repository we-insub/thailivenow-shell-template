# Frequently asked questions: UK to Thailand flights, airports and disruption help

Answers for UK travellers flying to Thailand, checked against airline and airport official sources on 13 October 2026. Where a fact could not be confirmed from an official source, the answer says so.

## Flights and routes

**Which airlines fly direct from the UK to Thailand?**
Five airlines operate UK–Thailand direct routes with airline-official confirmation: Thai Airways (London Heathrow–Bangkok), British Airways (London Gatwick–Bangkok), Norse Atlantic (London Gatwick–Bangkok, Manchester–Bangkok, and a new Gatwick–Phuket service starting December 2026), and TUI Airways (Gatwick, Manchester and Birmingham to Phuket on winter charters). EVA Air's London–Bangkok operation appears only on independent flight trackers and is treated as unverified.

**Does British Airways still fly from Heathrow to Bangkok?**
No. BA's Heathrow–Bangkok flights (BA9/BA10) have been withdrawn; Bangkok is served year-round from London Gatwick only, per BA's official announcement — three times a week in summer 2026, six a week in winter 2026–27.

**Is there a direct flight from Manchester to Bangkok?**
Yes — Norse Atlantic's Manchester–Bangkok service, the first direct link between the two cities, launched in November 2025. The airline's official announcement confirms an expansion to four flights a week from December 2026; the expanded timetable was not yet published at the time of checking.

**Can I fly direct from Manchester or Birmingham to Phuket?**
Yes, on TUI Airways' winter-season charter flights, which TUI lists in its official Thailand flight guide. Flight numbers and timings are confirmed through TUI's booking system rather than a public timetable.

**Are there direct flights from Edinburgh, Glasgow or other UK airports to Thailand?**
No direct service from Edinburgh, Glasgow, Newcastle, Stansted, Luton, Bristol or Cardiff to Thailand is confirmed from any airline's published material. All checked itineraries from those airports connect somewhere first.

**When does Norse Atlantic's Gatwick–Phuket flight start?**
It was announced for December 2026 through mid-April 2027, three times a week, but had not started operating as of 13 October 2026, with no flight numbers published.

## Check-in and counters

**Where does Thai Airways check in at Bangkok Suvarnabhumi?**
International economy at H6–H17, Royal Silk at A3–A10, and online-baggage counters at D5–D9 and J5–J19, per THAI's official airport-information page. Check-in opens 8 hours before departure and closes 50 minutes before; bags are accepted from 60 minutes before at the earliest. Its FAQ separately lists internet check-in counters J1–J5/H1–H5.

**Where does Thai Airways check in at Heathrow?**
THAI's official training material places check-in in Terminal 2 (The Queen's Terminal), Zone A, Level 5 — but the material dates from 2019, so reconfirm before you travel.

**Where does British Airways check in at Gatwick?**
From the dedicated Zone A check-in area in the South Terminal, a short walk from the Gatwick Airport railway station: 29 check-in kiosks and 12 self-service bag drops, with a separate space for premium customers.

**Where is EVA Air's ticketing counter in Bangkok?**
At EVA's Bangkok city office, not inside the airport terminal: 725 S-METRO Building, 17th Floor, Room 1700, Sukhumvit Road — Monday to Friday 08:30–17:30, phone +66-2-3027300.

**Does TUI publish its check-in counter numbers?**
No. TUI sells through its website, app and shops and does not publish fixed counter numbers for Gatwick, Manchester or Birmingham; check the departure screens on the day.

## Baggage and PIR

**Where do I file a PIR at Bangkok airport for Thai Airways?**
With THAI's ground staff at the arrival airport immediately, or at the THAI Baggage Services Office on Level 2 in front of the domestic arrivals exit. Keep the report copy and File Reference Number; the international Lost and Found line is +66-2134-5453~56 (24 hours).

**How quickly must I claim for damaged baggage with EVA Air?**
Report immediately on arrival, then claim within 7 days of receiving the baggage for partial loss or damage, or within 21 days of the scheduled arrival date for delayed or totally lost baggage, per EVA's official baggage page.

**Where are the baggage-service counters at Phuket airport?**
On the 1st floor of the international arrivals hall: Thai Airways near carousel No.5 and AOTGA near carousel No.1, per the airport's official arrival procedure.

## Delays, cancellations and insurance

**Can I get a delay certificate from British Airways, TUI, Thai Airways or EVA Air online?**
No official published procedure for written delay or cancellation certificates could be found on any of these airlines' own channels as of 13 October 2026. Ask the airline's ground staff at the airport for written confirmation on the day.

**What are my rights if my UK–Thailand flight is cancelled?**
Under UK passenger-rights rules, a cancelled covered flight normally entitles you to choose between a full refund for the unused journey and re-routing at the earliest opportunity. Care (meals, accommodation) applies during qualifying delays; fixed compensation depends on distance and arrival delay when the disruption was within the airline's control. The UK Civil Aviation Authority's official guidance on flight delays and cancellations is the reference point.

**What documents do I need for a UK travel-insurance baggage claim?**
At minimum: the airline's Property Irregularity Report, boarding pass and baggage tags, receipts for emergency purchases, proof of purchase for lost or damaged items, and photos of any damage. Some UK insurers insist on receipts for all items or items above a set value, so keep them from the start — that is the advice of MoneyHelper, the UK's government-backed money guidance service.

**What should I carry from my insurer while travelling in Thailand?**
Your policy number and the insurer's emergency assistance telephone number, per FCDO guidance, with a copy left with your travel companions and family at home.
